# On-device NFT wallet

The owner replaced Thirdweb custom JWT wallet authentication with a recoverable wallet generated on the iPhone. Firebase continues to authenticate the tagtag account; it does not authenticate to a wallet provider. No Firebase ID token is sent to Thirdweb.

The iPhone samples 128 bits from the operating system cryptographic random generator and encodes them as a 12-word BIP-39 English mnemonic with checksum. BIP-39 PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 and BIP-32 derive the Ethereum private key at `m/44'/60'/0'/0/0`. The phrase is saved under the Firebase UID in iOS Keychain with `kSecAttrAccessibleWhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly`. It is never written to app files, Firestore, Cloud Run, logs, or the chain. The user can explicitly reveal the words from the Account wallet screen and import them on another phone. Locking the phone makes Keychain reads unavailable; that must not be interpreted as a missing phrase.

The existing backend wallet-binding challenge remains the authority for selecting a mint recipient. The app reads the bound address before opening a local wallet. If the backend has an address and the local phrase is missing or derives a different address, it shows recovery required and does not generate or bind a replacement. Import validates BIP-39 checksum and, when bound, checks the derived address before saving the phrase. Multiple Firebase users on a shared phone have separate Keychain entries. Sign-out discards the in-memory signer, while leaving the phrase in Keychain. The backend still prevents cross-account address reuse and silent replacement.

The bundled Thirdweb SDK is used for Sepolia RPC and contract ABI encoding with a public client ID. Bundled Nethereum signs EIP-191 binding messages and EIP-155 transfer transactions locally. Neither Thirdweb In-App Wallet nor Thirdweb JWT authentication is used. Mint gas remains sponsored by the backend worker; transfer gas is paid from the user's phone address.

Before live rollout, verify the derivation against published BIP-39/BIP-32 vectors and an independent Ethereum tool, test restore and mismatch behavior, compile the native Keychain bridge, and confirm on a physical phone that the wallet binds and an app discovery mints to its address. Then restore the same phrase on a second phone and verify the address remains identical. The recovery phrase used in live testing must not be printed in logs or committed.
